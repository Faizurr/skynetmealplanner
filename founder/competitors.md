Researched 11 October 2026 for the idea in `founder/idea.md`. Every row is in

## Headline: the board was told something that turned out wrong

The board brief said that outside the US and English, almost nobody does this.
**That is wrong.**
- **Genspark's "Call for Me"** places AI calls in the local language in 7
  countries: the US, UK, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, China and France.
  - Twilio's case study reports 180,000 unique users and about 800 calls a day.
  - 23% of those calls use real-time translation.
  - Sources: [Genspark](https://genspark.ai/tools/ai-phone-call),
    [Twilio](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/genspark).
- **Small apps** such as TaraAI's *AI Call* claim hands-off calling plus
  translation in 100 or more languages
  ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6744344005)).
- **Pine** is better reviewed than the brief implied: 4.5 on the App Store from
  144 ratings and 4.3 on Google Play from 128
  ([search summary of the listings](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pine-ai-assistant-agent/id6746403769)).
  The brief only cited its 6 Trustpilot reviews.

The idea is still alive, but the gap is narrower than the board assumed (see
section 5). The board's verdict should be read with this correction.

## 2. Price range for the closest thing: getting one call done

The pricing models differ (per minute, per hour, monthly, credits), so this
compares what one typical 10-minute call would cost.

- **Lowest: free.**
  - A friend.
  - The phone's built-in translation, though you still do the talking.
  - Genspark's daily free credits: about 1.5 minutes a day at the unofficial rate.
  - Doctolib, for doctors who are on it.
- **Median (paid AI): about $20 a month.**
  - Google AI Pro is $19.99. Pine's premium starts at $19.
  - Agent AI's $5.99 a week works out to about $26 a month.
- **Highest: human help.**
  - A phone interpreter at €20 to €30 for 10 minutes (€1.99 to €2.99 a minute).
  - Red Tape Translation's €119 hour.
- **Our starting guess:** $2.99 per completed call, or $12.99 for 10. That
  undercuts every human option and sits below the paid AI plans.
  `/founder-pricing` tests it.

## 3. Positioning map

The two axes that matter to an expat:
- **Covers my country and language:** from US/English only to my country, in
  local language.
- **Does the whole job:** from "you still talk" to "hands-off, outcome
  delivered".

```
                         DOES THE WHOLE JOB (hands-off)
                                   ^
   Google Call for Me  Pine        |        Genspark (7 countries, no DE)
   Fancy Hands (human)             |        TaraAI AI Call, Dropcall (claims, tiny)
                                   |        Xpat24 (DE, no visible traction)
                                   |        >>> the target spot: DE expats, outcome-paid <<<
 US / ENGLISH ONLY ----------------+------------------------- MY COUNTRY, LOCAL LANGUAGE
                                   |
   Apple Hold Assist (waits only)  |        Apple Live Translation (5 call languages)
                                   |        Samsung Live Translate (13 languages)
                                   |        Phone interpreters €1.99-2.99/min, €119/h
                                   |        A German-speaking friend
                                   v
                         YOU STILL DO THE TALKING
```

The top-right quadrant is not empty. It holds:
- one general AI agent with real users (Genspark), which does not list Germany;
- a handful of tiny apps with unproven claims;
- one Germany-specific launch (Xpat24) with no visible traction.

## 5. The gap

**There is no empty market.** "An AI calls a business for me, in its language"
is already offered by:
- a general AI agent with 180,000 users (Genspark);
- a funded US specialist with good app-store ratings (Pine);
- Google, as a US preview;
- a dozen small apps.

On the evidence, four things are not done well:

1. **Germany, for expats.** Genspark's 7-country list does not include Germany
   ([Genspark](https://genspark.ai/tools/ai-phone-call)), and Google is US-only.
   Only Xpat24 claims the niche, and it shows no visible traction. Germany is the
   strongest launch country found so far. The same check is still needed for the
   Netherlands, Spain and Italy.
2. **Paying only for results.** The loudest complaint across the leaders is
   paying (in credits or subscriptions) for attempts that failed, with support
   that doesn't answer. **"You pay only when it's booked"** answers the #1
   complaint directly and is easy to understand.
3. **Knowing the local system.** For doctors, the best answer is often not a call
   at all: Doctolib is free, in English and covers many practices. A product that
   books on Doctolib when it can, calls when it must, and redials until it gets
   through feels to the user like "it just gets done". No AI caller found
   advertises this routing.
4. **Calls that aren't doctors.** Doctolib covers doctors and nothing else.
   Nobody focuses on expats' other phone calls:
   - landlords and building managers;
   - tradespeople;
   - health insurers;
   - telecoms;
   - vets, salons and car services.

**The gap is narrow and time-limited.** It is a place to win first (one country,
one group of people, paid on results, local know-how), not a place nobody will
follow.

## 6. The threat: who could copy this fastest

1. **Genspark.** It already has the agent, the translation and Twilio telephony
   in 40+ countries ([Twilio](https://customers.twilio.com/en-us/genspark)).
   Adding Germany is a configuration change, not a new product. Its weakness is
   billing and support trust, not technology.
2. **Doctolib.** It owns both ends of doctor bookings in Germany: the patient app
   and, through Aaron.ai, the AI that answers calls at 3,500+ practices
   ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/05/22/doctolib-acquires-aaronai/)). It could
   make phoning doctors pointless. Stay out of its way: route doctor bookings
   through it, and win on everything else.
3. **Google.** Its Call for Me already works; extending it to Germany and German
   would reach every Android user. No timeline has been announced
   ([TechCrunch via Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/18624/gemini-call-for-me-pixel-11-phones-stores)).
4. **Xpat24, or any developer with Vapi or Retell.** The calling stack takes
   weeks to copy. The defence is not the code. It is:
   - the local know-how (which offices take calls, what they ask, who speaks
     English);
   - trust built by charging only for results;
   - being known first in expat communities.

## What this changes for the rest of the pack

- **`/founder-consumer`:** the objections to test now include:
  - "I'll just use Doctolib";
  - "Genspark or ChatGPT does this";
  - "I don't trust an AI with my health insurance details";
  - "What if it books the wrong time?";
  - "I've been burned by credit-based apps before."
- **`/founder-pricing`:** the comparisons are:
  - free (Doctolib, a friend);
  - about $20 a month for AI plans;
  - €2 to €3 a minute and €119 an hour for human interpreters.
- **The board's checklist still stands.** Add one item: **pick the launch country
  from those Genspark and Google don't serve. Germany leads, but check the
  Netherlands, Spain and Italy first.**
