# Face recognition: engine choice, operation, limits

## Engine evaluation

| Option | Licence for commercial use | On-device / offline | Cost | Notes | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenCV YuNet + SFace** | MIT (YuNet), Apache-2.0 (SFace model + OpenCV) | Yes, CPU, Android via OpenCV AAR | Free | 128-d embeddings; same API in Python/Java, so the pipeline can be tested off-device; ~39 MB model | **Chosen** |
| Google ML Kit face detection | Free, Google terms | Yes | Free | Detection/landmarks only, **no recognition** | Not sufficient alone |
| InsightFace (ArcFace) pretrained models | Pretrained weights are **non-commercial** research licence | Yes | Licence needed | Higher published accuracy | Rejected (licence) |
| FaceNet community ports / MobileFaceNet weights | Weight provenance and training-data licences unclear | Yes | Free | Commonly used, licence risk | Rejected (licence uncertainty) |
| AWS Rekognition / Azure Face | Commercial | No, cloud only; Azure Face identification is gated | Per-call fees | Sends face images to a cloud provider; needs connectivity | Rejected (privacy, cost, offline) |
| Commercial SDKs (e.g. Regula, Innovatrics, FaceTec) | Paid licence | Yes | Licence fees | Certified liveness options | Recommended upgrade path if certified anti-spoofing is required |

Licensing note: the SFace model is distributed in OpenCV Zoo under Apache-2.0. Pretrained face models are trained on
public face datasets whose own terms vary. Have the model provenance reviewed as part of the legal review before
production (listed in [SECURITY_PRIVACY.md](SECURITY_PRIVACY.md)).

## Pipeline (identical on kiosk and in `tools/face-eval`)

1. Employee taps CHECK IN or CHECK OUT. Only then is the camera opened.
2. Frames go to YuNet (confidence ≥ 0.8). Zero faces keeps guiding the employee. More than one face is refused
   ("one person at a time"). A face smaller than 80 px gets "come closer".
3. Aligned crop (`alignCrop`, 5 landmarks), then SFace gives a 128-d embedding, L2-normalised.
4. 1:N match against this restaurant's active templates (each employee has ~5): employee score = best cosine similarity.
   Accept only when **score ≥ `match_threshold` (default 0.50)** and **score − best other employee ≥ `match_margin`
   (0.06)**, on **3 consecutive agreeing frames**. Otherwise retry, then offer the PIN fallback, and report an unknown face.
   The system never picks the "nearest" employee when unsure.
5. The kiosk submits the embedding (not the image). The server repeats step 4 with its own copy of the templates and
   rejects any disagreement.

## Measured results (pipeline sanity check, not an accuracy benchmark)

See [FACE_EVAL_RESULTS.md](FACE_EVAL_RESULTS.md), generated by `python3 -I tools/face-eval/eval.py`. On 17 public photos of
6 people: same-person cosine 0.544–0.976, different-person −0.107–0.304. At threshold 0.50 with margin 0.06 (leave-one-out
1:N): 16 identified, 1 rejected (asked to retry), **0 wrong-person acceptances**. Multi-person photos were all detected as
multiple faces. These are good-quality photos of a few people. They do **not** predict accuracy on your kiosk.
**No recognition test has been run on physical Android hardware yet.**

## On-site evaluation before go-live (recommended procedure)

1. Mount the tablet at its final position at face height (about 1.4–1.6 m), away from strong backlight (windows behind staff).
2. Enrol every employee in Manager mode (5 samples each). Then run "Test recognition" for each one.
3. For one week, have each employee use CHECK IN/OUT normally. Watch the dashboard for `not_recognized` retries
   (audit log `attendance.rejected`), unknown-face alerts, and PIN fallbacks.
4. If genuine staff are often rejected, improve lighting first. Lower `match_threshold` only in small steps (for example
   0.50 → 0.45), never below 0.40, and keep `match_margin` ≥ 0.05. If two employees look alike (siblings), increase
   `match_margin` or have them use the PIN.
5. Re-enrol anyone whose appearance changed significantly (beard, glasses, after a long absence).

## Liveness / anti-spoofing (software-only, limited)

Implemented:
- **Active head-turn challenge**: a random direction (left/right), measured from YuNet landmarks (nose offset ÷ eye distance
  beyond ±0.18, then back to centre), within 8 s.
- **Face continuity**: embeddings must stay consistent through the challenge, so a face cannot be swapped mid-check.
- **Static-box rejection**: a perfectly still bounding box (photo held up) fails.
- **Server enforcement**: when `liveness_required` is on, the server refuses events without a completed challenge.

Limits (be explicit with the owner):
- A replayed **video** of the employee turning their head, a good **3-D mask**, or a cooperating colleague holding a phone
  showing a video can defeat software-only checks on an ordinary RGB camera.
- These defences make casual buddy-punching with a printed photo or a still image on a phone hard. They do **not**
  guarantee protection against determined fraud.
- Mitigations: kiosk placed in view of staff or CCTV; review unusual patterns in the dashboard; optional upgrade to a
  certified liveness SDK (ISO/IEC 30107-3 PAD tested) or a depth/IR camera device.

## Privacy by design

- No raw images are stored by default. The optional reference photo is consent-gated, small (JPEG ≤ 200 KB), and
  deletable on its own.
- Templates are encrypted at rest on the server (AES-256-GCM) and on the kiosk (Android Keystore key). They are sent only
  to authenticated kiosks of the same restaurant, over TLS.
- Templates are deleted on request, on deactivation (offboarding), and on re-enrolment (old templates destroyed).
- Embeddings are never used to train models and are never shared between restaurants.
